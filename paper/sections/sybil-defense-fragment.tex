\paragraph{Closing the Sybil gap.}\label{sec:sybil} The ninth self-audit case is now a defense rather than a demonstration. Ranking collapses near-clone entries before it measures $\sigma_{\text{trials}}$: two pooled return streams are joined when their absolute cosine similarity reaches a dedicated clone-collapse constant of $0.995$, clusters are the connected components of that relation, and each cluster votes exactly once, with its median Sharpe, both in the dispersion estimate and in the count checked against the five-agent measurement floor. Clones are still scored and still appear on the board; they only lose their vote on the bar. The constant is deliberately not the rediscovery screen's $0.97$, because the two serve different purposes: rediscovery flags a similar strategy for review, while the collapse removes duplicate votes and must never silence an honest agent that is merely collinear with another. Honest agents are collinear. Across the nine evidence fields of this paper the largest honest pair is $0.990$ (buy-and-hold against a long-only luck-floor agent on weekly US indices; $0.981$, $0.978$ and $0.971$ on daily, weekly and 4-hour crypto, and $0.975$ between two luck-floor agents on daily US indices), so at $0.97$ five of the nine fields would have lost votes and the weekly US-indices field would have dropped below the measurement floor altogether. Sock puppets, by contrast, are copies: the audit's 200 puppets sit at cosine $0.99999$ or above against each other, while the borderline agent they are meant to help sits at $0.934$ against them and the honest field at $0.884$ or below pairwise. At $0.995$ (about $5.7$ degrees) the collapse merges nothing honest and every puppet. The partition and the median depend on the streams alone, so the result is order-independent, and a field with no clones measures byte for byte as before: the committed golden fixtures confirm this, and a harness regression test rebuilds every committed evidence field (the nine-dataset sweep, the risk-managed run and the pass witness) and asserts zero merges at the collapse constant, so the evidence in this paper is unchanged by the defense. The case scores the same field twice. With the collapse switched off the exposure reproduces: 200 puppets shrink the measured per-period dispersion of a seven-agent honest field from $0.3258$ to $0.0559$ and lift the borderline agent's DSR from $0.000$ to $0.9756$, admitting it. With the default on, the same 200 puppets are worth one vote: the field measures $0.3018$, bit-identical to a field containing the honest seven plus the cluster's median puppet, the agent's DSR stays at $0.000$, it remains refused, and all 200 puppets are still on the board. The rediscovery diagnostic still flags 199 of 199 puppets as clones of the first. A regression test asserts both halves, so a future change that either stops the attack from working with the collapse off or lets it through with the collapse on fails the build; \texttt{sharpebench audit} reports all nine attacks demoted and no known gaps. What the collapse does not do is bound the estimate from below or verify identities: an adversary who submits genuinely dissimilar low-dispersion streams (cosine under $0.995$ against every other entrant, which about ten percent of independent noise on each copy achieves) can still compress the measured dispersion, so a configured floor on $\sigma_{\text{trials}}$ and per-identity entry caps remain the mechanical next steps.

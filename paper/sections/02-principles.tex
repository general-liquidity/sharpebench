\section{Design principles}\label{sec:principles}

Every decision in the benchmark traces to one of seven principles. They are stated here so that the rest of the paper can be read as their consequences, and so that a reader who disagrees with a decision can locate the principle it follows from.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Luck-robust.} A score that does not deflate for the number of strategies tried is a score of the best overfitter. The deflated Sharpe ratio is a ranking gate, not a reported extra, and the number of trials an agent declares raises its own bar.
\item \textbf{Reliability-gated.} An agent that is safe on average is not safe. Eligibility requires clearing the per-run bar on every execution seed and every out-of-sample window, which is a stronger claim than having an edge and is meant to be.
\item \textbf{Process-gated.} A return earned by placing an order that skipped the risk gate, ignoring a drawdown halt, or exploiting the simulator is not a return. One block-severity violation in any run zeroes the entry regardless of profit.
\item \textbf{Point-in-time.} The agent interface can express only history at or before the decision. Look-ahead is prevented at that boundary, and the boundary is the thing to audit.
\item \textbf{Deterministic.} The kernel reads no clock, draws no ambient randomness, performs no input or output, and sums in a fixed order. A score is a pure function of a submission and a configuration, byte-identical on any platform, and the continuous-integration matrix checks that rather than assumes it.
\item \textbf{Forward-attested.} Trust comes from a commitment made before the data exists and a signed chain anyone can verify, not from the host's good word. The host can compete on its own board.
\item \textbf{Judge-free.} No model grades another model. Every gate is an assertion over numbers, which is what makes the self-audit in \cref{sec:selfaudit} a property of the code rather than an opinion about it.
\end{enumerate}

Two consequences of these principles are worth stating before the experiments, because the experiments are about them. First, principle 1 requires a dispersion of Sharpe ratios across trials, and the literature supplies that quantity annualized while the kernel computes Sharpe per period. Principle 5 makes the mismatch visible, since the scorer is reproducible enough to be wrong in exactly the same way everywhere. Second, principles 2 and 3 together mean the benchmark can decline to certify any agent at all on a dataset, including the market itself. That outcome is not a failure of the benchmark. Whether it is the right outcome is a question the paper puts to the data.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

SharpeBench is an evaluation protocol that treats a trading agent's score the way a statistician would: deflated for search, required to survive every seed and window, zeroed on process violations, and tested against a serial-correlation-preserving null, with every number recomputable from committed data by a listed command. Applied to an author-written field of reference agents across nine frozen dataset-timeframe combinations, roughly five dependent market panels, the gates refused all 72 agent-dataset verdicts and gave a reason naming the gate and the quantity each time. That refusal is a weak test of skill: under serially independent normal returns the all-weather gate on six windows passes an agent with a true annualized Sharpe of $1$ less than 2 percent of the time and one of $2$ about half the time, and on three panels the deflation gate cannot discriminate within the field (\cref{sec:power,sec:passk}). In a single common-random-number draw, a controlled injected edge is first admitted between annualized Sharpe $2.9$ and $4$ on these window geometries, so the bar is strict rather than unsatisfiable; where that boundary lies beyond the one draw awaits replication over independent draws. A benchmark-relative verdict, which asks only for outperformance of same-window buy-and-hold, refuses every entrant as well, so the refusal is not an artifact of the all-weather mandate. Four rules whose specifications come from the published literature, implemented here and scored under each of three cost profiles, refuse in all 351 cells, and removing every trading friction admits nobody either, so the refusal is not an artifact of the cost calibration; what the cost profile does move is the deflation bar itself. Risk management proved frequency-dependent in the same field, improving worst-window drawdown on seven of nine panels and amplifying it on the two where the trend signal churns, which a per-window score keeps visible. The thousand-agent floor leaves every zero-skill entrant ineligible, and the ninth audit case, which showed that field-measured deflation was not Sybil-resistant, is now demoted by the clone collapse that closed that gap. One of the paper's five numbered findings is a correction to the benchmark itself, three further defects found while writing are recorded in \cref{sec:limitations}, and the repairs made after the evidence was frozen are recorded in \cref{app:repairs}; the general lesson of that first finding, that a deflation gate is only as correct as its unit conventions, applies to any scorer that mixes annualized literature constants with per-period statistics. The report-only prospective surface extends the same refusal discipline to forecast contracts, proper scores, pairwise support and dependence-aware comparison without allowing forecast quality to buy trading rank. The protocol and kernel are the contribution. What the paper still lacks is a ceiling it did not build itself: no external entrant and no published rule has cleared the bar, so attainability rests on one draw of a synthetic witness. A current-model field and the resolution of \texttt{window-003}, the open forward window bound to the attested v0.9.0 scorer artifact, are the two tests that would change that.

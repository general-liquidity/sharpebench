### Strongest Counter-Argument

Grant the paper its genuine strengths first: the units finding is honestly reported against the author's own interest, the reproducibility apparatus is unusually complete, and the inverse comparison table is a rare act of fairness. Here is how a hostile scholar would still dismantle it.

The paper titles itself a benchmark for AI trading agents, yet no AI agent appears in it. Every entrant is a deterministic rule the author wrote: buy-and-hold, momentum, hold, five random baselines, and one risk-managed strategy the author also wrote, added expressly to defend the benchmark against the vacuousness charge. The central empirical result, that zero agents are eligible anywhere, is therefore compatible with two readings the paper cannot separate: the gates are discriminating, or the conjunction is jointly unsatisfiable by any real strategy at the shipped defaults. The paper's own Table 2 says that even after the units fix, a daily agent needs roughly a 2.5 annualized Sharpe at N equals 50, Medallion territory, and the deflation bar itself is set by the measured Sharpe dispersion of a field that is five ninths random agents. The one agent offered as proof of discrimination is refused at a trial count of 50 that it never incurred, with no sensitivity at N of 10 or the honest field size reported. Meanwhile the pass-k finding is close to a tautology, long-only strategies lose money in bear windows, and it is contingent on sample windows that happen to contain 2020 and 2022. Finally, the benchmark's authority argument, determinism plus a self-audit, is refuted by its own first finding: the audit stayed green for the entire life of a benchmark whose bar was off by a factor of up to sqrt(8760). What survives is careful infrastructure and an honest bug report, not yet evidence that this benchmark measures skill.

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths

1. Jointly unsatisfiable predicate, not discriminating gates. Universal refusal across 576 cells is equally predicted by a conjunction whose defaults (DSR 0.95 at N 50, per-run PSR 0.90 in every window) no realistic strategy on these panels can satisfy simultaneously. The paper's own Table 2 shows the post-fix daily bar near 2.5 annualized Sharpe. One author-built agent failing one gate does not separate the hypotheses; a family of synthetic agents with known injected edge, swept over edge size, would, and the deterministic kernel makes that experiment cheap. It was not run.
2. Sample-period artifact. Every "bear window" driving Finding 2 comes from 2020 and 2022 sitting inside every frozen range. The structural-sounding claim "the market itself cannot pass" is a claim about one decade's windows.
3. Baseline-zoo artifact. Measured sigma-trials of 0.070 comes from a field dominated by random agents; the DSR verdicts, including the risk-managed agent's 0.30, are conditional on that arbitrary field composition.
4. Simpler design not considered: reporting DSR with a bootstrap interval and letting the reader pick thresholds, rather than a hard eligibility conjunction. The paper never argues why certification (a binary gate that currently certifies nothing) beats calibrated reporting for the stated audience.

### Missing Stakeholder Perspectives

- Agent developers: a board on which nothing has ever ranked offers no gradient; nothing is said about why anyone would enter.
- Capital allocators: the predicate refuses the equity risk premium that most allocated capital deliberately holds; whether "safe to hand capital" is their construct is never asked.
- Rival benchmark authors: compared via their own self-statements without the re-scoring the adapter exists for.
- LLM-agent research community: the population the title names, entirely absent from the evidence.

### Unexamined Premise

The paper assumes a benchmark's purpose is certification, a binary gate an agent must pass before capital, rather than comparison, a ranking that orders progress. All four theses inherit this premise. Under the comparison purpose, a board with zero eligible entries has produced no output at all for its community, and the paper's defense ("this is the gate working") is a defense of the certification premise, never of the premise itself.

### Observations (Non-Defects)

- Reporting the units bug, the false gating-documentation claim, and the HMAC verifiability correction as findings rather than silently fixing them is genuinely to the paper's credit and rare in benchmark papers.
- The inverse comparison table (tab:related-inverse) with its own row empty is an honest device other benchmark papers should copy.
- The evidence directory with committed JSONL records and a single reduction script (analyze.py) makes this review's checks possible; that is the reproducibility standard working as intended.
- The drawdown-multiplicativity correction in sec:ablation (a per-run bound at the pooled value catches nothing) is a nice piece of self-scrutiny.

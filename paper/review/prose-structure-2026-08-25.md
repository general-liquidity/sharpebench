# SharpeBench manuscript: prose and structure review

Read-only review of `paper/main.tex` and every file under `paper/sections/`, including the five
`\input` fragments. Line numbers are file lines; most LaTeX paragraphs here occupy a single long
line, so `05-experiments.tex:107` means "the paragraph on line 107".

Detectors applied: `no-ai-slop`, `humanink` (+ PATTERNS), `anti-slop`, `humanizer`,
`scientific-writing`, `research-paper-writing`, `every-style-editor`, `skimmable`,
`paper-narrative`. They are used as detectors only. Where a pattern they flag is correct usage in
this genre (measured hedging in Limitations, repeated technical terms such as DSR / pass^k /
deflation, parallel column structure in results tables, enumerated kernel properties), it is
recorded as **legitimate** and left alone.

Headline: this is a well-argued, unusually honest manuscript whose prose has one dominant tic and
one structural defect. The tic is the *closing verdict kicker*: roughly one paragraph in three ends
on a short antithetical sentence that restates the paragraph it closes. The structural defect is
that the abstract and introduction are organized by the order in which the author discovered
things, not by what the reader needs first.

## 1. Abstract

File: `sections/00-abstract.tex`, one paragraph, 442 words.

### 1.1 Diagnosis against context / gap / approach / result / implication

| Move | Present? | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Context (why anyone should care) | **Missing** | Opens on `"Benchmarks for trading agents often rank realized return over short windows"`. The subject is benchmarks, not the world. Nothing says agents are being handed capital. |
| Gap (what no one has done) | **Implicit only** | The FinBen counterexample stands in for the gap. The abstract never states that no trading-agent board deflates for selection or gates on reliability. |
| Landscape (where this sits) | **Missing** | No mention that finance solved the statistics decades ago, that agent evaluation solved the reliability half (pass^k), and that this work is the join. A reader cannot place the contribution. |
| Approach | Present, but as a spec dump | `"a deterministic, judge-free scoring kernel whose rank predicate has five conjuncts"` followed by the five, comma-listed. |
| Results | Present, but seven of them, ordered by discovery chronology | units bug, then repaired records, then the 4,608-cell refusal, then risk-managed control, then witness, then audits, then luck floor, then arena bookkeeping. |
| Implication | **Missing** | Ends on `"Every reported number is generated by a listed command from committed data and a content-hashed source snapshot."` That is a reproducibility statement, not an implication. |

### 1.2 Specific structural faults

1. **The confidence interval lands before the reader knows why it matters.** By the first semicolon
   (word ~40) the reader is holding `$1.51 \pm 1.08$` and `$0.02 \pm 0.87$` with no reason to care.
   Evidence beats stakes into the room.
2. **The five-conjunct predicate is delivered as a list, not a claim.** The claim ("skill, not
   return, decides rank") is buried in the four-word sentence that follows: `"Raw return never
   ranks."`
3. **Arena bookkeeping does not belong in an abstract.** `"Two empty pre-entry forward-window
   records were explicitly superseded when scoring provenance changed"` is section-4 provenance
   detail. The scope-honest part of that sentence (window open, no forward result claimed) should
   survive; the supersession history should not.
4. **Numbers no reader can use out of context.** `"an intentionally unfloored diagnostic peaks at
   DSR $0.2500$, while the operational dispersion floor lowers the maximum to $0.0012$"` requires
   three definitions the abstract has not given.
5. **Length.** 442 words against a NeurIPS convention of roughly 200. Cutting faults 3 and 4 gets
   most of the way.

### 1.3 Proposed replacement abstract

Same facts, same scope honesty (no LLM agent evaluated; window-003 open and unresolved; no forward
result; author-written entrants only). Do not edit the file from this report; this is the draft for
the author to place.

> Autonomous agents are starting to allocate real capital, and the boards that rank them mostly
> rank realized return over a single short window. At that horizon sampling noise, selection across
> many configurations, and unpriced tail risk can dominate skill: one widely cited board reports its
> leading model at a Sharpe ratio of $1.51 \pm 1.08$ against a buy-and-hold baseline at
> $0.02 \pm 0.87$, intervals that overlap. Finance answered this question with deflation for
> multiple testing, and agent evaluation answered the reliability half with $\text{pass}^{k}$; no
> trading-agent benchmark has combined them. SharpeBench is a deterministic, judge-free scoring
> kernel that ranks on a five-part eligibility predicate: deflation for the number of strategies
> tried, $\text{pass}^{k}$ across execution seeds and out-of-sample windows, a process audit, a
> stationary block-bootstrap null, and the configured mandate. Raw return is reported and never
> ranks. We validate the protocol on author-written entrants, three reference agents, a
> risk-managed control, seeded zero-skill agents, and a synthetic family with a controlled injected
> edge, over nine frozen datasets spanning four asset classes and four bar sizes. No LLM or learned
> agent has competed and no frontier-model result is reported. Three results follow. First, a
> dimensional trap in the benchmark itself: an annualized deflation prior applied per period demanded
> annualized Sharpes of $18$ on daily bars and $106$ on hourly ones, and every self-audit stayed
> green throughout, so a deflation gate is only as correct as its unit conventions. Second, with
> units repaired, no agent is eligible in any of 4{,}608 scored cells under any of three reliability
> verdicts, each refusal reported with the gate and quantity that produced it. Third, a synthetic
> witness places the acceptance boundary near annualized Sharpe $3$ on one-to-two-year windows, so
> the predicate is strict and satisfiable. Until a competing agent clears it, the artifact is best
> read as a refusal-diagnosis tool: the first forward window is open, holds no commitment, and
> resolves after this paper.

Word count 289. If a harder cut is wanted, the sentence beginning "Until a competing agent clears
it" can absorb the scope statement and the preceding "No LLM or learned agent has competed"
sentence can be dropped, giving 265.

What changed and why: stakes added (capital, autonomous agents); landscape added in one sentence
(finance + agent evaluation, the two literatures being joined); results cut from seven to three and
ordered by importance rather than by discovery date; arena supersession history removed; implication
added as the closing sentence, which also carries the scope honesty rather than leaving it as an
isolated disclaimer. `$0.2500$ / $0.0012$`, the 1,000-agent floor, the nine attacks, and the
risk-managed control are all still in the paper; none of them is the abstract's job.

## 2. Introduction

File: `sections/01-introduction.tex`.

### 2.1 CARS move analysis

**Move 1 (establish territory)** is present but interrupted at sentence two. Line 3 opens:

> `Give a thousand random agents a quarter of market data and one of them will appear exceptionally skilled.`

Good opener: concrete, no jargon, sets the whole paper's problem in one image. Then immediately:

> `The thought experiment is old; this paper's falsification leg runs a seeded version of it under the shipped cost model (\cref{sec:falsify,sec:luck1000}), where trading costs make every zero-skill track lose money before any gate sees it, so the leg shows the floor staying below the bar rather than a lucky tail being refused at it.`

This is a Move-3 methods caveat and a Limitations concession, inserted as sentence two of the
territory move. It defuses the opening image before the image has done any work, and it forward-
references two sections the reader has no map for. **The move order is wrong here.** The caveat is
correct and must stay in the paper; it belongs in `sec:falsify` (where it already appears in
substance at `07-limitations.tex:13`) or at the end of the paragraph.

**Move 2 (establish the niche)** is present and well done: the finance citations, then
`"The benchmarks now used to rank LLM trading agents inherit none of that caution."`, then the two
exhibits, then the evidence map. This is the strongest passage in the paper.

**Move 2b (stakes)** arrives at the end of line 3 and is the best writing in the manuscript:

> `For a coding agent a flattering benchmark wastes some time. For an agent that allocates capital it selects the strategy most likely to blow up, the one whose backtest looked best because it overfit hardest.`

Keep verbatim. This is what the abstract is missing.

**Move 3 (occupy the niche)** at line 5 is correctly placed and correctly sized.

**Move 3b (scope)** at line 7 is unconventional but right for this paper. Putting the "no LLM has
competed" concession before the findings, rather than in Limitations only, is what makes the
findings readable as protocol behavior rather than market claims. Keep the position.

**Move 4 (roadmap)** is absent, which is acceptable at NeurIPS. But see 2.3.

### 2.2 The findings list is well placed; the contributions list is not

The four bolded `itemize` findings (lines 9-13) are the paper's real story and are correctly placed
after the scope statement. The problem is what follows.

`01-introduction.tex:16` `\paragraph{Contributions.}` restates the same material a third time (after
the abstract and after the findings list). Overlap is near-total:

- Contribution (1) restates finding-list preamble and line 5.
- Contribution (3) `"Evidence on the protocol's dimensional calibration, refusal diagnoses, a synthetic pass witness and a corrected one-symbol luck floor"` is a table of contents for findings 1-4, which the reader just read.
- Contribution (5) `"A direct simulation of the 1{,}000-agent luck-floor tail invoked by the opening thought experiment, on two daily datasets"` is a hardening experiment, not a contribution at the level of (1) and (2).
- Contribution (6) `"An import adapter that turns re-scoring a rival board's published field under these gates into one command; no rival demotion is claimed until such a field is actually re-scored"` is a contribution that explicitly claims no result. Listing infrastructure that produced nothing, with the disclaimer attached, weakens the list.

**Replace `01-introduction.tex:3`, sentences 1-2**, moving the falsification caveat out:

> Give a thousand random agents a quarter of market data and one of them will appear exceptionally

> variance of noise more than it measures skill. Finance established this long ago: [rest of the

The 1,000-agent floor and the import adapter keep their sections; they lose their contribution-list
slots.

## 3. Every other section

### 3.1 Design principles (`02-principles.tex`)

**Rhetorical job:** give the reader the axioms so the rest reads as consequence. **Does it?**
Partly.

- **Opening:** `"Every decision in the benchmark traces to one of seven principles, stated here so the rest of the paper reads as their consequences."` The trailing clause is over-signposting; the section title already says this.
- **The seven principles occupy one paragraph in one shape.** Each is `\textbf{Name}: aphorism, so consequence.` Seven times, in a single unbroken paragraph. This is the manuscript's clearest case of robotic rhythm and it also fights the reader: seven load-bearing definitions arrive with no visual separation. `skimmable` flags this as the highest-cost formatting decision in the paper. Fix: `\begin{description}` or `\begin{itemize}`, one principle per item. That change alone raises the section's usefulness more than any rewording.
- Three of the seven are stated as negations rather than definitions: `"a score that does not deflate for the number of strategies tried is a score of the best overfitter"`, `"an agent that is safe on average is not safe"`, `"a return earned by bypassing a risk control is not a return"`. Individually each is sharp. Three in a row is a pattern.
- **Closing:** `"That is not a failure of the benchmark; whether it is the right outcome is a question the paper puts to the data."` Defensive and personified. Replace with: `"Whether universal refusal is the right outcome on a given history is a question \cref{sec:experiments} puts to the data."`
- The determinism-versus-validity passage (`02-principles.tex:5`) is genuinely good and load-bearing. Keep.

### 3.2 The benchmark (`03-benchmark.tex`)

**Rhetorical job:** state what is computed, in what units, and what decides rank. **Does it?** Yes,
and this is the most disciplined prose in the paper.

- **Opening is exemplary:** submission, scorer, contract, in three sentences. The fourth,
  `"This section states what the scorer computes, in the units it computes it, and which statistics decide rank."`, is deletable over-signposting; the three sentences before it already established that.
- **Internal ordering fights the reader once.** `\subsection{Statistics}` runs 25 lines of dense
  paragraph before `\subsection{The eligibility predicate}` gives `\cref{eq:eligible}`, which is the
  thing every one of those statistics feeds. The predicate is the section's spine and arrives after
  the ribs. Consider stating `eq:eligible` immediately after the section opener, then deriving each
  conjunct. The paragraph titles inside Statistics (`Probabilistic and deflated Sharpe`,
  `Execution replicates are not extra market time`, `Reliability: seeds and windows are different tests`) are good and do real navigational work.
- `sec:incentives` is the strongest sub-argument in the paper: it states a threat model against its
  own inputs and names what is unbuilt. Keep as is.
- **Closing** of `sec:simdata` trails off into data caveats after the strong nine-dataset statement.
  The `\cref{tab:data}` handoff is fine.

### 3.3 Threats to validity and integrity (`04-integrity.tex`)

**Rhetorical job:** enumerate how the benchmark could be gamed or mislead, and what a reader can
check. **Does it?** Yes.

- **Opening:** `"A benchmark that scores capital allocation is a target."` Punchy and portable. It
  passes the portability test only barely (it would fit any benchmark paper). Consider naming the
  attacker: `"A benchmark that certifies capital allocation invites entrants who optimize against it rather than against the market."`
- The second sentence, `"This section lists the ways it could be gamed or could mislead, and for each says what the benchmark does and how a reader can check."`, is over-signposting. The paragraph titles show the structure.
- **Ordering fault:** `sec:selfaudit` opens with a `\subsection` command and then runs the section's
  body on the same line, so the prose begins immediately after the label. It reads fine compiled but
  the source pattern (`\subsection{Self-audit}\label{sec:selfaudit} Benchmark-integrity studies find...`) recurs at `sec:attest` and makes both subsections start on a citation rather than on a claim. Prefer opening each with the claim, then the citation support.
- `sec:attest` is one 400-word paragraph carrying six distinct ideas (commitment scheme, Ed25519
  chaining, lifecycle, config digest, the two superseded windows, window-003, operator custody). It
  needs paragraph breaks; as written the window-003 fact, which the abstract and conclusion both
  point at, is buried mid-block.
- **Fragment integration:** `sybil-defense-fragment.tex` reads as integrated prose and is placed
  correctly (right after the self-audit claim it supports). No seam.

### 3.4 Experiments (`05-experiments.tex`) and fragments

**Rhetorical job:** four numbered findings plus hardening. **Does it?** Yes, and the findings
ordering is right (the benchmark's own bug first, then the headline refusal, then the ablation, then
the discriminating control).

- **Opening paragraph** is a clean methods block. Keep.
- `sec:demo` closes on `"The rest of this section asks what happens on the market."` Over-signposting; delete.
- **Two mini-roadmaps inside one section.** `\paragraph*{The other two reliability verdicts.}`
  (line 109) and `\paragraph*{Hardening the experiment.}` (line 114) both exist only to announce what
  the next subsections do. Subsection titles already do this. One transition paragraph is defensible;
  two is signposting the signposts. Keep line 109 (it makes a real point: verdict fixed / agent
  varied, then the reverse) and cut line 114 to a single clause or delete it.
- **The closing-kicker habit is concentrated here.** See slop list items 1-12.
- `sec:riskmanaged` (line 107) ends by restating line 105 almost word for word, with an authorial
  note left in the text (`"a result the summary should not drop"`). See item 4.
- **Fragments:** `relative-mandate-fragment.tex` and `mandate-declaration-fragment.tex` read as
  integrated prose and follow the section's `Definition / Evidence / Result / Command` shape
  consistently. `execution-noise-fragment.tex` also integrates but its `\paragraph{Interpretation.}`
  is the longest interpretive block in the paper and duplicates the `\paragraph{Result.}` conclusion.
  `hardening-fragment.tex` is a single 250-word paragraph that ends on a kicker (item 8); it is the
  weakest-integrated of the five and could take a paragraph break before "Three scores distinguish".
- **Ordering that fights the reader:** `sec:perturb` (a diagnostic that, on real data, "separates
  nothing") sits between the mandate fragments and the witness. The witness is the section's second
  most important result after finding 2. Consider moving `sec:witness` before `sec:perturb`.

### 3.5 Related work (`06-related.tex`)

**Rhetorical job:** place the contribution and state the trade honestly. **Does it?** Yes, better
than most. The two-table device (own axes, then rivals' axes) is genuinely good practice.

- The aphorism `"A comparison drawn only on one's own axes is advertising"` appears twice, verbatim
  in body text (line 7) and in the `tab:related` caption (line 16). Keep the caption instance, cut
  the body instance, or vice versa.
- Line 9 opens `"What the individual rivals rank on is the reason the axes look the way they do."`
  Inverted and abstract. Replace: `"Each rival's ranking metric explains one of the axes."`
- Line 11 closes the section's argument well and honestly.
- The final paragraph (`Evaluation as a scientific object`) is placed last but is the paragraph that
  most directly justifies the paper's construct. Consider moving it before `Benchmarks for financial agents` so the reader meets the reliability-versus-capability frame before the comparison tables that encode it.

### 3.6 Limitations (`07-limitations.tex`)

**Rhetorical job:** bound every claim. **Does it?** Emphatically, and this is the paper's ethical
core. Almost nothing here should be cut; the hedge density flagged by the detectors is **legitimate**
scientific register in this section.

- Two faults only. First, `"and is listed first on purpose"` (line 3) is metadiscourse about the
  document's own ordering; the reader can see it is first. Second, the section repeats the
  selected-histories caveat that already appears at `01-introduction.tex:11` and `05-experiments.tex:61` (see item 43).
- **Closing:** the Conclusion is a `\section` inside the Limitations file. That is a build detail,
  not a reading problem, but it means `main.tex` has no `\input` for a conclusion, which will confuse
  the next person editing structure. Consider `sections/09-conclusion.tex`.
- The Conclusion itself opens well (`"treats a trading agent's score the way a statistician would"`)
  and closes on the right note (the two open tests). Its middle recapitulates the findings list a
  fourth time.

### 3.7 Reproducibility (`08-reproducibility.tex`)

Does its job in two paragraphs. The LLM-disclosure paragraph is exactly right in tone. No changes.

### 3.8 Appendices

- `A-commands.tex`: the prose between verbatim blocks is uniformly functional. `A-commands.tex:65` is
  the exception, a 70-word sentence carrying the witness's methodological guarantee, which is too
  important to live only in an appendix caption-like note.
- `B-checklist.tex`: the `Partly` / `Yes` / `Partly, and the gap is named` answers are the right
  form. `"Yes, and it is the paper."` is a rhetorical flourish in a checklist; replace with `"Yes. \Cref{sec:experiments} traces every refusal..."`.
- `C-simdata.tex`: closes `sec:data` on `"A realism gate that never fails is not a gate."` (item 6).
  `app:unitsfix` is the best-written appendix passage; keep.

## 5. Voice

Single-author paper. Four registers are in use, and they alternate without a rule.

- **First person plural**: exactly one instance, `00-abstract.tex:2` - `"We validate the protocol on author-written reference agents..."`.
- **The paper as agent**: 33 instances of `this paper` / `the paper` doing things, e.g. `03-benchmark.tex:29` `"The paper therefore calls the default every-run requirement a \emph{regime-robustness mandate}"`; `03-benchmark.tex:39` `"The paper does not claim the shipped default is the right definition of safe; it claims the default is conservative and inspectable."`; `07-limitations.tex:15` `"The paper records four limitations of earlier protocol configurations"`.
- **The benchmark or kernel as agent**: `04-integrity.tex:35` `"loading rejects a mismatch"`; `02-principles.tex:5` `"the benchmark can decline to certify"`. Mostly correct and idiomatic for describing software behavior.
- **Agentless passive for authorial acts**: 40+ instances, concentrated where the author made a
  choice, e.g. `05-experiments.tex:99` `"a risk-managed reference agent was added to the field"`;
  `05-experiments.tex:124` `"so a witness was constructed"`; `05-experiments.tex:91` `"the kernel was extended so that both are expressible from configuration"`; `03-benchmark.tex:45` `"The crypto and index symbol sets were selected with hindsight"` (`07-limitations.tex:7`).

The drift is systematic in one direction: **whenever the author made a judgment call, the sentence
goes passive or hands agency to "the paper."** That is exactly the place a reviewer wants to see who
decided. `"a witness was constructed"` hides the most consequential design choice in Section 5.

**Recommended convention (pick one and apply throughout):**

> Use **"we"** for authorial acts (we built, we scored, we chose, we validate, we do not claim).
> Use **"the kernel" / "the scorer" / "ranking"** for software behavior (the scorer converts, ranking
> collapses clones). Reserve **"this paper"** for structural references only (`"\Cref{sec:witness} of this paper"`), and never as the subject of a verb of judgment.

Worked conversions:

- `03-benchmark.tex:39` `"The paper does not claim the shipped default is the right definition of safe"` -> `"We do not claim the shipped default is the right definition of safe."`
- `03-benchmark.tex:29` `"The paper therefore calls the default every-run requirement a regime-robustness mandate"` -> `"We therefore call the default every-run requirement a regime-robustness mandate."`
- `05-experiments.tex:124` `"The reference field cannot separate them, so a witness was constructed."` -> `"The reference field cannot separate them, so we constructed a witness."`
- `05-experiments.tex:99` `"a risk-managed reference agent was added to the field"` -> `"we added a risk-managed reference agent to the field."`
- `05-experiments.tex:91` `"the kernel was extended so that both are expressible from configuration"` -> `"we extended the kernel so that both are expressible from configuration."`
- `07-limitations.tex:7` `"The crypto and index symbol sets were selected with hindsight"` -> keep passive? No: `"We selected the crypto and index symbol sets with hindsight from assets that survived into the collection period."` The admission is stronger with an agent.
- `04-integrity.tex:35` `"loading rejects a mismatch"` -> unchanged (software behavior).

If the venue or the author prefers to avoid "we" in a single-author paper, the alternative is
consistent agentless passive plus zero personification of "the paper", which means rewriting all 33
"the paper does X" instances. That is more work and reads colder. Recommend "we".

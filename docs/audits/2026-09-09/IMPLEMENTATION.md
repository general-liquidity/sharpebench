| G28 | Read the sources directly and build what the transfer review left open | Merged | The previous round acted on an outside assessment. This one read the five Sharpe-ratio papers and William Sharpe's own 1994 article against the code, and built the five extensions that assessment classified as present-but-extendable and that no round had picked up. Three claims in the product's own prose did not survive the reading, all in Bench PR #103, main `781880c`. The manipulation-proof measure was described as something that cannot be raised by selling tail risk. That is unconditional and it is false, and the repository's own test streams falsify it: with the loss realized the tail seller still wins at every risk aversion below `1.644367729837862`, a flip point now pinned rather than bracketed, and in any sample where the loss has not yet landed it wins at every risk aversion at all, because the measure is a sample average and an unrealized tail is invisible to one. What Goetzmann, Ingersoll, Spiegel and Welch prove is that an uninformed investor cannot expect to raise his estimated score, at a risk aversion solved against the benchmark, which this kernel does not solve. The claim is now stated in the register the probabilistic ratio's claim was narrowed to a round earlier, as a statement about the risk aversion and the evaluation point. Second, the field naming the null of the significance test was documented as an annualized benchmark Sharpe, which invites an operator to enter an index's ratio and receive a test of something else: against a benchmark earning six percent at fifteen percent volatility, a fund earning eight at twenty has a differential ratio of `0.217` and a difference of ratios of exactly zero.
